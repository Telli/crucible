- Studio is now one full-screen viewport with floating controls and icons, its handles follow the object's own axes, the selected object gets an outline, undo takes back one whole drag and leaves the camera alone, and the colours and fonts come from the brand kit. Studio is no longer a page of the public site: it stays an addon that an app embeds, with a development page under `bench/studio`. When an agent edits the scene, a pointer with the agent's name, in a colour of its own, travels to each object it changed. The viewport gains working aids: a ground grid in metres with the X and Z axes, a view widget that snaps to the main views, and a readout of the camera and the size of the selection. Move handles end in arrows, and Studio opens on a plain blockout scene of three grey primitives. Turning a ring shows where the turn began and a faint wedge over the angle turned so far. A drag leaves a dashed outline where the object was, a move also draws the line it has travelled, and a new object lands beside the ones already in the scene. Studio is split into three workspaces that follow the work from left to right, Build, Look and Animate, each showing only its own panels, so the timeline no longer covers the readout; scene rows draw the shape of each object, a selected row has one highlight, and the toolbox gaps are even. The Scene card moves to the right with Properties under it, across from the tools; Studio gains Blender keys (Shift+A, Shift+D, X, Esc, 1/3/7, N, I) and a Settings dialog for the snap step and angle, handle size, starting workspace and what the viewport shows, kept in the browser (#2177). The top bar now has File, Edit, View, Agent and Help menus with the key beside each row, the handle under the pointer lights up, the dashed drag trace shows on any surface and an axis that points at the camera stays long enough to grab. The Scene and Properties column sits on the right, across from the tool rail, the Scene head has a search button and an add button, the Add menu gains disc, ring, arch, vase, star, 3D text and an area light, handle heads show a border only under the pointer, and the top left shows the Crucible mark. The selection outline now follows the object's shape, F flies the camera to the selection in a third of a second, the grid covers the floor and changes its step with the zoom, and zooming far out keeps the floor and the scene in view. The add menu has thirteen more shapes (capsule, dome, pyramid, prism, hexagon, wedge, pipe, gem, heart, bowl, spring, knot and stairs), each an ordinary recipe geometry, shown in groups. The panel column can be resized by dragging its inner edge and the gap between its cards, each card folds down to its head, the rail has one line between the tools that change the scene and the switches that show or hide things, and Export is a plain white button. Floating panels can be turned off in the View menu or in Settings: the bar, the rail and the column then dock to the window edges and the scene is drawn in the room they leave, at its true proportions.

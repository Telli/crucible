- Studio is now one full-screen viewport with floating controls and icons, its handles follow the object's own axes, the selected object gets an outline, undo takes back one whole drag and leaves the camera alone, and the colours and fonts come from the brand kit. Studio is no longer a page of the public site: it stays an addon that an app embeds, with a development page under `bench/studio`. When an agent edits the scene, a pointer with the agent's name, in a colour of its own, travels to each object it changed. The viewport gains working aids: a ground grid in metres with the X and Z axes, a view widget that snaps to the main views, and a readout of the camera and the size of the selection. Move handles end in arrows, and Studio opens on a plain blockout scene of three grey primitives. Turning a ring shows where the turn began and a faint wedge over the angle turned so far. A drag leaves a dashed outline where the object was, a move also draws the line it has travelled, and a new object lands beside the ones already in the scene. Studio is split into three workspaces that follow the work from left to right, Build, Look and Animate, each showing only its own panels, so the timeline no longer covers the readout; scene rows draw the shape of each object, a selected row has one highlight, and the toolbox gaps are even. The Scene card moves to the right with Properties under it, across from the tools; Studio gains Blender keys (Shift+A, Shift+D, X, Esc, 1/3/7, N, I) and a Settings dialog for the snap step and angle, handle size, starting workspace and what the viewport shows, kept in the browser (#2177). The top bar now has File, Edit, View, Agent and Help menus with the key beside each row, the handle under the pointer lights up, the dashed drag trace shows on any surface and an axis that points at the camera stays long enough to grab. The Scene and Properties column sits on the right, across from the tool rail, the Scene head has a search button and an add button, the Add menu gains disc, ring, arch, vase, star, 3D text and an area light, handle heads show a border only under the pointer, and the top left shows the Crucible mark. The selection outline now follows the object's shape, F flies the camera to the selection in a third of a second, the grid covers the floor and changes its step with the zoom, and zooming far out keeps the floor and the scene in view. The add menu has thirteen more shapes (capsule, dome, pyramid, prism, hexagon, wedge, pipe, gem, heart, bowl, spring, knot and stairs), each an ordinary recipe geometry, shown in groups. The panel column can be resized by dragging its inner edge and the gap between its cards (floating, each card resizes on its own by its left and bottom edges, Ctrl resizes both, and the axis widget moves to the top right beside the column), each card folds down to its head, the rail has one line between the tools that change the scene and the switches that show or hide things, and Export is a plain white button. The bar, the rail and the column dock to the window edges by default and the scene is drawn in the room they leave, at its true proportions; Floating panels in Settings puts them over a full-window scene instead. G, R and S now pick the selected object up under the pointer as Blender does: X, Y or Z keeps it to one axis, a click places it, Esc puts it back, and a pill names those keys while you hold it. A right click on an object, in the viewport or the Scene list, opens a menu of its actions, H hides the selection and Alt+H shows everything again, and Alt+G, Alt+R and Alt+S reset its position, rotation and scale. Menu rows carry an icon where one fits. The move, rotate and scale handles answer a press within 11 pixels of an axis or ring and 16 pixels of its tip, while the drawn handle stays thin.
- Studio: on a scene whose camera a motion track moves, orbit, zoom, pan, F and the view presets now move a free view of the viewport, where they used to do nothing; View → Animated camera returns to the animated camera.
- Studio: the scene camera, the sun and every light are now objects in the Scene list with marks in the viewport; click to select, drag a light with the Move tool, press Delete to remove a light, and the controller gains `listSceneObjects`, `selectSceneObject`, `moveSceneObject` and `deleteSceneObject`.
- Studio: a selected light, the sun and the camera now have property panels laid out like Blender's Object Data tab (light color, intensity, radius, range, shape, shadows and position; sun strength, color and size; camera focal length, sensor, clip planes, shift, depth of field, eye and target). Every edit is one undo step, fields a motion track drives are locked and marked, and a change an agent makes shows in the controls; the controller gains `updateSceneLight`, `updateSceneCamera` and `describeSceneObject`.
- Studio: the Look tab now edits every environment, effect and shadow setting an agent can write, laid out like Blender's World, Render and Film properties: the HDRI, sky, daylight, fill and ambient strength, fog and volume fog, exposure, temperature, vignette, grain, ambient occlusion, bloom, depth of field, motion blur, the shadow box and the render quality switches. Property groups fold and stay as you left them, fields a motion track drives are locked and marked, and a change an agent makes shows in the controls; the controller gains `updateSceneShadow`.
- Studio: the property panels are one line per property, as in Blender: label on the left, control in a value column on the right, switches for toggles, a slim slider with its value for bounded numbers, a smaller sun pad beside its label and lens presets inside the Lens group. Group titles stay at the top while their rows scroll and only the first group of a tab starts open. Angles show in degrees, lengths in metres and lens values in millimetres as a quiet unit, numbers show three decimals at most, an HDRI the list lacks shows as Custom and its first characters, Properties takes the rest of the column below a Scene card of at most 35 percent, and the column scrollbars are thin (#2177).
